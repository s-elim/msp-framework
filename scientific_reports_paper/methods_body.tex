
\subsection{LM-Grasp: real observations with simulated grasp outcomes}
\paragraph{Images and splits.} We use every real test image of the 13 objects of the standard LINEMOD protocol~\cite{hinterstoisser2012} in the BOP release~\cite{hodan2018bop} (bowl and cup are excluded, as in that protocol), \NScenes\ RGB-D images in total, each showing one annotated target object in clutter. The rendered BOP training images are not used. Images are split per object into blocks of 50 consecutive frames, and each block is assigned to train, validation, calibration or test with probabilities 0.55, 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20 (\NTrain, \NVal, \NCalib\ and \NTest\ scenes), so near-duplicate neighbouring frames never straddle two splits. Every method observes a square RGB-D crop around the ground-truth visible-mask bounding box (side 1.4 times the longer box side), resized to $96\times96$ pixels, with depth expressed relative to the median object depth, divided by 0.25\,m and clipped to $[-1,1]$. Using the annotated mask is the usual known-detection assumption of LINEMOD pose studies; it is shared by all methods, including FoundationPose, which receives the same mask.

\paragraph{Candidate grasps.} Each scene receives 16 parallel-jaw grasp candidates built from the observation alone. A plane fitted to the depth pixels around the object gives the table normal; each candidate picks a visible object point (with probability 0.7 from the top 5\,cm of the visible surface, otherwise from all visible points), an approach direction tilted from straight down by up to $45^\circ$, a penetration depth of 0--3\,cm past the surface point, a minimum height of 4\,cm above the table and a uniformly random closing-axis angle. A candidate is encoded relative to the visible-point centroid as its translation and the first two columns of its rotation matrix (a continuous six-dimensional rotation representation~\cite{zhou2019rotation}). No ground truth enters the proposal.

\paragraph{Simulated execution.} Each candidate is mapped into the object frame with the ground-truth pose and executed in MuJoCo~\cite{todorov2012mujoco} against a convex decomposition (CoACD~\cite{wei2022coacd}, concavity threshold 0.05, at most 24 parts) of the object's scanned LINEMOD mesh, placed upright on a table (the model $+z$ axis deviates from the table normal by a per-object median of \UprightDegLo--\UprightDegHi$^\circ$ on the test poses). A floating parallel-jaw gripper with a 138\,mm opening between the finger pads, 70\,mm fingers and 10\,N closing force per finger closes for 0.6\,s, holds for 0.2\,s and lifts by 12\,cm at 0.15\,m\,s$^{-1}$; the grasp succeeds if the object ends at least 5\,cm above its resting height. A candidate whose open jaws already intersect the object or the table is not executable and counts as a failure in every metric. The friction coefficient, mass and centre of mass of each scene are drawn independently of the image: friction from a log-normal distribution with median 0.5 and log-scale s.d. 0.4, mass log-normal around a nominal value (convex-part volume times 500\,kg\,m$^{-3}$, clipped to 0.05--2\,kg) with log-scale s.d. 0.4, and the centre of mass from an isotropic Gaussian with s.d. 0.15 times the object's half-extents. LINEMOD does not publish object masses, so these distributions are a prior rather than measured properties. The resulting corpus contains \NRollouts\ rollouts; on the test split, \TestExec\ of candidates are executable, \TestBase\ succeed, and \TestOracle\ of scenes contain at least one successful candidate. The images are real; every grasp outcome is simulated, and no grasp in this study was executed on a robot.

\subsection{Methods compared}
\paragraph{Manipulation-sufficient perception.} The framework learns, from the observation $o$ alone, a statistic $z$ from which the success probability of any candidate action $a$ can be read off, $p(y{=}1\mid o,a)\approx g_\phi(z,a)$. All learned scorers share one architecture: an ImageNet-initialised ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet,deng2009imagenet} with a four-channel RGB-D stem maps the crop to a 256-dimensional feature and then to a 32-dimensional code, and an outcome head maps $(z,a)$ to a success logit and two auxiliary outputs (a zero-inflated log-normal slip distance and a Gaussian contact margin, each weighted 0.01 in the loss); in the head, $z$ modulates a 256-unit action embedding by feature-wise scale and shift before three 256-unit layers. One code scores all 16 candidates. The \emph{stochastic} instantiation (MSP) encodes a diagonal Gaussian belief $q_\theta(z\mid o)$ and minimises
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{(o,a,y)}\Big[-\log \tfrac{1}{K}\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{K} p_\phi(y\mid z_k,a)\Big]+\mathrm{KL}\big(q_\theta(z\mid o)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\big),\quad z_k\sim q_\theta(z\mid o),
\end{equation}
an action-conditioned variational information bottleneck~\cite{tishby2000ib,alemi2017vib} with the marginal (log-mean-exp) outcome likelihood over $K=8$ reparameterised draws~\cite{kingma2014vae}; $\beta$ sets the trade-off between outcome distortion and rate. At test time the success score of a candidate is the posterior mean over 32 draws. The \emph{deterministic} instantiation is the same network trained with the rate term removed and $z=\mu_\theta(o)$; its payload is reduced after training by principal component analysis. It was planned as the ablation of the rate term and is reported as an instantiation of the framework because it is the stronger one on this benchmark. The deep ensemble~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017} averages five deterministic scorers trained from different seeds; three disjoint ensembles (seeds 0--4, 5--9, 10--14) give its seed variability.
\paragraph{Grasp-patch baseline.} A network with the layer stack of the Dex-Net 2.0 grasp-quality CNN~\cite{mahler2017dexnet} (convolutions 64@7, 64@5, pooling, 64@3, 64@3, pooling, a 1024-unit layer, merged with a scalar stream carrying the grasp depth relative to the object and the three-dimensional approach direction) scores a $32\times32$ depth patch aligned with each candidate's jaw axis, cut from the same crop. It is retrained on LM-Grasp with Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$), as in Dex-Net; it receives the same candidates and labels. We use its architecture, not the released Dex-Net weights, which were trained for a different gripper and sensor.
\paragraph{Pose-then-plan.} A pose pipeline estimates the object pose, places the object's mesh in the simulator at that pose and scores every candidate by its success rate under four physics draws from the same prior (the nominal draw plus three samples), so the planner knows the object identity, its scanned mesh, the simulator and the physics prior, but not the scene's friction, mass or centre of mass. Three pose sources are used: (i) a learned pose regressor (the same ResNet-18 with a head predicting a six-dimensional rotation and the translation offset from the visible centroid, trained with a symmetry-aware loss on the same crops); (ii) FoundationPose~\cite{wen2024foundationpose} with its official pretrained weights, the full-resolution RGB-D image, the ground-truth visible mask, the camera intrinsics and the LINEMOD CAD model, five refinement iterations (its LINEMOD setting); and (iii) the ground-truth pose, a privileged reference that bounds every pose pipeline using this planner.

\subsection{Training protocol}
All learned methods use the same crops, candidates and labels, 20 epochs, batch size 64 and identical training-time augmentation (per-image brightness and contrast scaling of $\pm20\%$, per-channel gain of $\pm5\%$, and Gaussian depth noise of s.d. 2.5\,mm on valid pixels). The ResNet-based methods use AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} (learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, cosine decay; the scorers add a two-epoch linear warm-up); MSP anneals the rate term over three epochs after a two-epoch deterministic warm-up. Each method keeps the checkpoint with the lowest validation loss of its own decision score among epochs 5--19 (success cross-entropy for the scorers, pose loss for the regressor). Every configuration is trained with five seeds (main comparison) or three seeds (ablations and unseen objects) on one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU.

\subsection{Metrics}
\emph{Top-1 success} is the simulated outcome of the highest-scoring candidate of a scene, averaged over test scenes (ties broken by a fixed pseudo-random jitter shared by all methods). \emph{Within-scene AUC} is the area under the ROC curve among the candidates of one scene, averaged over the \NMixed\ test scenes that contain both outcomes. \emph{Selective success} at coverage $c$ is the top-1 success on the fraction $c$ of scenes with the highest score of the selected candidate. The \emph{certificate} is split conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005,lei2018,angelopoulos2023} calibrated on the selected candidate of each calibration scene with nonconformity $1-\hat p$ for successes and $\hat p$ for failures: a test scene is certified when the prediction set of its selected candidate is $\{\text{success}\}$; we report the certified fraction and its precision at miscoverage $\alpha=0.1$. Pose accuracy is the fraction of test scenes with ADD below 10\% of the object diameter~\cite{hinterstoisser2012}, with ADD-S for the symmetric eggbox and glue~\cite{hodan2016eval}.

\subsection{Payload}
The payload is the entropy-coded size of the transmitted code: each transmitted coordinate is quantised uniformly to $b\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ bits on the range between its 1st and 99th calibration percentiles, and the code length is the cross-entropy of the test symbols under per-coordinate symbol frequencies estimated on the calibration split (add-one smoothing), the size a static entropy coder fitted before deployment would spend. To vary the payload, each method sends $m\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$ coordinates ranked by its own calibration-split criterion, fixed before any test result of this scheme was computed: MSP sends the means of the $m$ dimensions with the largest average KL divergence to the prior, and the receiver uses the prior for the remaining dimensions and the calibration-mean log-variance for the sent ones; the deterministic code sends its first $m$ principal-component coefficients. Decisions are recomputed from the dequantised code with the trained head.

\subsection{Pose payload}
For comparison with the learned codes, the annotated test poses are coded as a translation in millimetres and a rotation vector in degrees, each coordinate quantised with a common step of 1, 2, 5 or 10 units and entropy coded with per-coordinate symbol frequencies fitted on the calibration poses (add-one smoothing), the same estimate as for the codes. A uniform step $\Delta$ has a per-coordinate error s.d. of $\Delta/\sqrt{12}$, which places the planner's success at that resolution on the pose-error curves of Fig.~\ref{fig:noise}.

\subsection{Pose-error study}
To measure how pose error translates into grasp failure, the pose-then-plan planner is run with the ground-truth pose perturbed by a rotation about a uniformly random axis with angle $|\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_R^2)|$, $\sigma_R\in\{1,2,5,10,20,45\}^\circ$, or by an isotropic Gaussian translation with per-axis s.d. $\sigma_t\in\{2,5,10,20,40\}$\,mm, three noise seeds each, on all test scenes.

FoundationPose errors are stratified by the ADD(-S) error in units of the object diameter, by the BOP visible fraction of the object and by object diameter (tertiles), with the exact-pose planner and the learned scorers evaluated on the same scenes.

\subsection{Unseen objects}
Ape, cat, duck and phone (drawn once with a fixed seed) are removed from training and validation; the methods are trained on the remaining nine objects with three seeds and evaluated on the test and calibration scenes of the four held-out objects. This tests generalisation to new object shapes, not to new grippers, sensors or real physics.

\subsection{Statistics}
We report mean $\pm$ s.d. over training seeds. Differences in top-1 success between methods are tested with a paired bootstrap over the \NTest\ test scenes (10,000 resamples) of the per-scene top-1 outcome averaged over each method's seeds; we report the mean difference with its 95\% percentile interval. Seed variability is reported separately and is not part of this interval. Differences in selective success, which is computed on method-specific subsets of scenes, are tested by resampling test scenes with replacement and recomputing both methods' selective success on each resample (2,000 resamples).
